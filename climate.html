2. Microclimate Drivers & Campus Heat Island Mitigation

Situated in the interior basin of Tamil Nadu surrounded by low granitic hills, Vellore experiences severe continental heat. With over 6.49 lakh square meters of built-up concrete surface, the campus would ordinarily generate a pronounced Urban Heat Island (UHI) effect.

Field measurements reported in the 2023 Green Audit demonstrate that VIT's urban forest (30,552 trees covering 18.69 acres, plus 1.19 lakh ornamental and medicinal shrubs) reduces ground-level shade temperatures by up to 3.0°C relative to peripheral asphalt roads.

Water-Energy Nexus in Campus Cooling

The 5,500 TR District Cooling System relies on evaporative cooling towers to reject condenser heat. Recognizing that regional groundwater is constrained, the university routes 100% of cooling tower make-up water from the campus Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) rather than tapping freshwater borewells or the TWAD Cauvery pipeline. This closed loop consumes nearly 1,200 KLD of recycled tertiary effluent during peak summer.

Innovative Cooling: Gandhi Block as an ICAP Model

To move beyond energy-intensive vapour compression, the Mahatma Gandhi Block incorporates passive radiant slab cooling combined with Indirect Evaporative Cooling (IEC). By embedding chilled water pipes directly within structural floor slabs, thermal mass absorbs internal radiant heat without requiring high fan speeds. While standard split ACs consume 97.48 kWh/m²/year, Gandhi Block operates at 48.79 kWh/m²/year—a verified 50% power saving.
