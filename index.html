1. Context & Investigation Background

Established in 1984 as Vellore Engineering College and granted university status in 2001, the Vellore Institute of Technology (Katpadi, Vellore) encompasses 372 acres (8,27,746.85 m²) in the Palar river basin. Operating at an academic scale comparable to a self-contained township, the campus accommodates 36,050 undergraduate and postgraduate students, 3,856 doctoral scholars, and over 7,200 faculty and administrative staff.

With 21,936 residential students residing in 32 hostel blocks (21 boys' blocks, 11 girls' blocks), campus utilities must maintain continuous electrical service, climate control across high-rise facilities, potable water distribution (7,233 KLD), and solid waste processing under the extreme climatic variations characteristic of northern Tamil Nadu.

2. Energy Transitions & Structural Load Realities

Between 2014 and 2019, campus electricity demand surged from 27.7 million kWh to 37.16 million kWh, driven by campus population growth and the commissioning of multi-storey residential towers exceeding 10 floors. The university's peak electricity crisis occurred in April 2019, when HT electricity consumption reached 46,88,760 kWh in a single month with an instantaneous maximum demand of 8,756.4 kVA against a 9,000 kW sanctioned threshold.

In response to grid shortages and mandatory rolling load-shedding during 2018–2019, VIT initiated three structural interventions:

  • Academic Calendar Realignment: In 2018, university leadership shifted the primary student summer vacation from December/June to May and June, commencing the winter semester early in December. This single scheduling shift saved an estimated 20 lakh units (2,000,000 kWh) per annum by ensuring high-density hostel blocks are vacated during Vellore's hottest weeks (when ambient temperatures regularly exceed 40°C).
  • Offsite Wind Power Purchase Agreements: Contracting 90 lakh units of wind energy wheeling via the TANGEDCO grid (partnerships with Bharat Enterprises and BBK Shoes), which pushed renewable energy share from 2.67% in 2018 to 18.11% in 2019, and ultimately 35.35% in 2024.
  • Centralized District Cooling (DCS): Deploying an 11-unit 5,500 TR centralized chilled water plant using R134a refrigerant, replacing inefficient split and window air-conditioners across hostels (Q, M, N, K, L blocks) and cutting cooling power consumption by approximately 42% compared to decentralised DX systems.

3. Microclimate Moderation & Campus Ecological Balance

Vellore experiences semi-arid tropical extremes with annual rainfall concentrated in the North-East Monsoon (800–900 mm). The campus maintains 5,84,005 m² of greenery and open unpaved land, including 30,552 trees and over 1,19,000 shrubs. Empirically measured shade temperatures beneath the campus canopy are on average 3.0°C lower than surrounding exposed bitumen and paved road corridors, creating a vital microclimatic buffer.

Furthermore, natural carbon sequestration calculations conducted in the 2024 Environmental Audit establish that campus flora and open soils absorb 2,814.44 tonnes of CO₂ annually (1,869 t from tree canopy, 876 t from soil respiration/porous ground, and 69.4 t from lawns and shrubs), providing an active 9.15% offset against the university's 30,757 tonne gross greenhouse footprint.

Methodology & Boundary Disclaimer
VIT Vellore operates with certified bulk metering at the 33/11 kV central substation and primary feeders across 12 powerhouses. Per-room and per-lab IoT sub-metering has not yet been deployed campus-wide. Throughout this report and accompanying maps, high-demand zones (e.g. SMEC, SELECT, CBST, CFM) are transparently modelled from transformer capacity, equipment manifests, and occupancy metrics. All modelled layers are visually demarcated.
