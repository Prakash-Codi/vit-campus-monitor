2. Anatomy of the 2018–2019 Peak Deficit & Interventions

Prior to 2018, VIT observed a summer vacation during December and June. However, energy audits demonstrated that December and January had the lowest baseline consumption (20–22 lakh units) due to mild winter weather and idle chillers. Conversely, April and May presented extreme cooling demand, pushing maximum demand to 8,756.4 kVA—dangerously close to the 9 MW sanctioned limit.

A. The 2018 Rolling Load Shedding Regime

During the severe summer grid deficit of 2018–2019, TANGEDCO imposed regional restrictions. VIT introduced a staggered 1-hour rolling load shedding rotation across Men's Hostels (A, B, D, E, F, G, H, J, K, L, M, N) from 09:00 to 22:00. Uninterrupted power for fans and essential lighting was sustained via 15 backup diesel generators (500 kVA each), burning thousands of litres of fuel.

B. Vacation Shift: The 20 Lakh Unit Saving

To permanently eliminate this vulnerability without compromising student comfort, the academic calendar was shifted in 2018. The summer vacation was permanently moved to May and June, with exams terminating around May 25. By closing hostel high-rises during peak heatwaves, May 2019 consumption fell to 17.6 lakh units and June to 14.8 lakh units, saving over 20 lakh units annually compared to pre-shift baselines.

C. COVID-19 Lockdown Shock (2020)

In late March 2020, nationwide lockdowns evacuated over 22,000 hostellers. In April 2020, campus consumption plummeted by 92% to 3,67,680 kWh, representing essential administrative baseload, server data centres, and water treatment operations.

3. Decarbonisation Trajectory: Grid vs Solar vs Wind (2014–2024)

From 2014 to 2018, VIT drew almost 100% of its electricity from the coal-heavy Southern regional grid. Commencing in 2018 with offsite wind wheeling and 2.113 MWp of rooftop solar PV, renewable procurement expanded to 16.12 million units by 2024.

Technical Notes & Footnotes
  1. April 2019 Bill Discrepancy: The Energy Audit discussion mentions 40 lakh units as peak consumption, but Table 'EB Bill Analysis' records 46,88,760 kWh and ₹3,44,13,114. We preserve the verified meter ledger value of 4.69M units.
  2. 2021 Renewable Percentage (103.66%): The 2020 Energy Audit records RES % = 103.659% for 2021. This reflects only 6 months of COVID-restricted campus consumption (27,42,960 kWh) against full wheeling credits (17,32,792 kWh wind + 11,10,534 kWh solar). This mathematical anomaly is documented here and excluded from annualised performance metrics.
  3. Tariff Structure: Electricity is billed under TNPDCL HT Tariff 1A (Educational Institutions) with demand charges at ₹562/kVA/month, energy charges at ₹6.75/kWh, and 20% Time-of-Day peak surcharge between 06:00–09:00 and 18:00–21:00.
